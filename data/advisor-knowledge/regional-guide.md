# Regional Farming Guide — Pakistan

## Overview
Pakistan's agriculture varies significantly by region due to climate, soil, water availability, and farming traditions. This guide covers the major agricultural zones with region-specific advice.

## Punjab — The Breadbasket

### Central Punjab (Faisalabad, Sahiwal, Lahore, Gujranwala)
- **Climate:** Semi-arid, hot summers (40–45°C), mild winters (5–15°C)
- **Soil:** Alluvial, fertile, well-drained
- **Water:** Canal irrigation + tube wells (groundwater declining)
- **Major crops:** Wheat, cotton, rice, sugarcane, maize
- **Key challenges:** Water table decline, urbanization encroaching farmland

**Regional advice:**
- Wheat sowing: November 1–20 optimal
- Cotton: Prefer Bt varieties for bollworm resistance
- Rice: Avoid flood irrigation — use raised bed or SRI method to save water
- Monitor for whitefly in cotton (peak: July–August)

### Southern Punjab (Multan, Bahawalpur, D.G. Khan, Rajanpur)
- **Climate:** Arid, extreme heat (45–50°C), low rainfall (<200mm)
- **Soil:** Sandy loam to loam, some saline areas
- **Water:** Canal irrigation, tube wells (saline groundwater in some areas)
- **Major crops:** Cotton, wheat, sugarcane, mango, citrus
- **Key challenges:** Heat stress, salinity, waterlogging in low-lying areas

**Regional advice:**
- Cotton sowing: April 15–May 15 (avoid late sowing)
- Mango: Apply potassium for fruit quality in saline soils
- Use gypsum for saline/alkaline soil reclamation
- Heat-tolerant wheat varieties recommended (Faisalabad-08, Galaxy-13)

### Northern Punjab (Sialkot, Jhelum, Rawalpindi, Murree)
- **Climate:** Sub-humid, moderate summers (35–40°C), cold winters (0–10°C)
- **Soil:** Loam to clay loam, well-drained foothills
- **Water:** Canal + rain-fed (barani) areas
- **Major crops:** Wheat, rice, maize, vegetables, citrus
- **Key challenges:** Flooding in monsoon, hailstorms in spring

**Regional advice:**
- Barani wheat: Sow October 15–November 10 (earlier than irrigated areas)
- Rice: Basmati varieties preferred, transplanting in June
- Vegetables: High-value opportunity for Sialkot, Gujranwala markets
- Protect standing crops from hailstorms (March–April)

## Sindh — The Indus Delta

### Upper Sindh (Sukkur, Larkana, Jacobabad)
- **Climate:** Arid, extreme heat (48–52°C — hottest in Pakistan), low rainfall
- **Soil:** Alluvial, some saline/sodic patches
- **Water:** Canal irrigation (Indus river system)
- **Major crops:** Rice, cotton, wheat, sugarcane, dates
- **Key challenges:** Extreme heat, waterlogging, salinity

**Regional advice:**
- Rice: Preferred crop — use IRRI-6 or Super Basmati varieties
- Cotton: Heat-tolerant varieties essential, sow by May end
- Dates: Major cash crop in Larkana/Khairpur — Deglet Nool and Aseel varieties
- Address waterlogging through proper drainage

### Lower Sindh (Hyderabad, Thatta, Badin)
- **Climate:** Arid to semi-arid, moderate heat, coastal influence
- **Soil:** Sandy, saline in coastal areas
- **Water:** Canal + rain-fed in some areas
- **Major crops:** Rice, cotton, wheat, mango, fisheries
- **Key challenges:** Salinity, cyclone risk, limited irrigation

**Regional advice:**
- Salt-tolerant rice varieties recommended (KS-282, KS-132)
- Mango: Sindhri variety — world-class quality
- Use drip irrigation for vegetables in saline areas
- Coastal areas: brackish water management is critical

## Khyber Pakhtunkhwa — The Highland Farms

### Peshawar Valley and Lowlands
- **Climate:** Semi-arid, hot summers (40–42°C), cold winters (2–8°C)
- **Soil:** Loam to clay loam, fertile valleys
- **Water:** Canal + tube wells, some rain-fed
- **Major crops:** Wheat, maize, sugarcane, tobacco, vegetables
- **Key challenges:** Limited water, terraced farming constraints

**Regional advice:**
- Wheat: Sow 1–2 weeks earlier than Punjab (October 20–November 10)
- Maize: Major crop — use hybrid varieties for higher yield
- Tobacco: Flue-cured variety — specialized knowledge needed
- Vegetables: High-value opportunity for Peshawar and Swat markets

### Malakand and Hazara Divisions
- **Climate:** Sub-humid, cool summers, cold winters with snowfall
- **Soil:** Mountain soils, variable fertility
- **Water:** Rain-fed + limited canal, snowmelt-fed streams
- **Major crops:** Wheat, maize, rice, fruits (apple, pear, walnut)
- **Key challenges:** Short growing season, frost risk, limited mechanization

**Regional advice:**
- Short-duration wheat varieties essential (120–130 days)
- Fruits: High-value orchard crops — apple, pear, walnut, apricot
- Terracing and water conservation critical
- Frost protection: Irrigate before cold nights

## Balochistan — The Frontier

### Quetta and Surrounding Areas
- **Climate:** Arid, extreme temperature variation (40°C summer, -5°C winter)
- **Soil:** Rocky, shallow, variable fertility
- **Water:** Rain-fed + limited tube wells
- **Major crops:** Wheat, barley, fruits (apple, grape, pomegranate, almond)
- **Key challenges:** Water scarcity, limited infrastructure, frost

**Regional advice:**
- Apple: Major cash crop — Kharak, Chitral, and Ornamental varieties
- Pomegranate: Drought-tolerant, excellent for dry areas
- Wheat: Rain-fed (15–20 maunds/acre) — supplemental irrigation doubles yield
- Water harvesting: Build small dams and check dams for rainwater storage

### Lasbela and Coastal Areas
- **Climate:** Arid, hot, coastal humidity
- **Soil:** Sandy, saline
- **Water:** Limited canal, tube wells (saline groundwater)
- **Major crops:** Dates, mango, vegetables, fisheries
- **Key challenges:** Salinity, water scarcity, heat

**Regional advice:**
- Dates: Excellent for Lasbela — Bermi and Aziz varieties
- Drip irrigation essential for vegetable production
- Use salt-tolerant crop varieties
- Coastal fisheries: Major livelihood source

## General Regional Tips

### Water Conservation (All Regions)
- Use drip or sprinkler irrigation where possible
- Line canals to reduce seepage losses
- Practice rainwater harvesting
- Schedule irrigation based on crop need, not calendar

### Soil Health (All Regions)
- Get soil tested every 2–3 years
- Add organic matter (compost, farmyard manure) annually
- Practice crop rotation — legumes after cereals
- Avoid over-tillage — conserve soil structure

### Market Access
- Know your nearest mandi and its price trends
- Use transport cost in sell decisions
- Consider value addition (grading, cleaning, packaging) before sale
- Connect with buyer networks for better prices
