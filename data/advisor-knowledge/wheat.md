# Wheat — Complete Crop Guide for Pakistan

## Overview
Wheat is Pakistan's staple food crop, grown on approximately 9 million hectares primarily in Punjab and Sindh. It is a Rabi season crop (sown October–December, harvested April–May). Major varieties include Akbar-19, Faisalabad-08, Galaxy-13, and Ujala-21.

## Crop Calendar

### Sowing
- **Optimal window:** November 1–25 for irrigated areas; October 15–November 15 for rainfed/barani areas
- **Late sowing:** Every day delayed after November 15 reduces yield by approximately 15–20 kg per hectare
- **Seed rate:** 120–140 kg per hectare (approximately 50 kg per acre)
- **Seed treatment:** Treat seed with fungicide (Vitavax or Topsin-M at 2.5g per kg seed) before sowing to prevent seed-borne diseases

### Key Growth Stages
| Stage | Days After Sowing | Critical Management |
|---|---|---|
| Germination | 0–10 | Ensure adequate soil moisture |
| Tillering | 20–45 | First irrigation critical; top-dress nitrogen |
| Jointing/Booting | 45–75 | Second irrigation; monitor for rust |
| Heading/Flowering | 75–95 | Most sensitive stage; avoid water stress |
| Grain filling | 95–120 | Third irrigation; monitor for aphids |
| Maturity/Harvest | 120–150 | Harvest at 12–14% moisture |

### Irrigation Schedule
1. **First irrigation (CRI — Crown Root Initiation):** 20–25 days after sowing. Most critical — missing this reduces yield by 20–30%.
2. **Second irrigation:** 40–45 days (tillering to jointing)
3. **Third irrigation:** 70–75 days (booting stage)
4. **Fourth irrigation:** 95–100 days (grain filling) — if needed

Apply 60–75 mm (approximately 2.5–3 acre-inches) per irrigation. Avoid irrigation when strong winds are forecast.

### Fertilizer Schedule
| Nutrient | Total Dose | Basal (at sowing) | Top Dress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N) | 120–150 kg/ha | Half (60–75 kg) | Half at first irrigation |
| Phosphorus (P₂O₅) | 60–90 kg/ha | Full (all at sowing) | — |
| Potash (K₂O) | 30–60 kg/ha | Full (all at sowing) | — |

**Recommended fertilizers per acre:**
- DAP: 1 bag (50 kg) at sowing
- Urea: 1.5 bags (75 kg) — half at sowing, half at first irrigation
- SOP (Sulphate of Potash): 25 kg at sowing (if soil test indicates deficiency)

**Zinc:** Apply zinc sulphate (25 kg/ha) at sowing if the field has not received zinc in the last 3 years. Zinc deficiency is widespread in Punjab soils.

## Diseases

### Yellow Rust (Stripe Rust)
**Symptoms:**
- Yellow-orange pustules (stripes) appear on leaves in parallel lines along the leaf veins
- Starts on lower leaves, spreads upward
- Most damaging at jointing to grain filling stages
- Favored by cool temperatures (10–15°C) with high humidity

**Cause:** Fungus *Puccinia striiformis*. Spreads via wind-borne spores from infected fields.

**What to do:**
1. Scout fields weekly from January through March
2. If yellow rust pustules are found on more than 5% of leaves, spray immediately
3. Apply fungicide: Nativo (tebuconazole + trifloxystrobin) at 300 ml per acre, or Tilt (propiconazole) at 250 ml per acre
4. Repeat spray after 15 days if disease persists
5. Use resistant varieties for next season (Galaxy-13, Ujala-21 have good resistance)

**When:** Most common January–February in Punjab. Check fields early morning when dew is present — pustules are easier to spot.

**Caution:** Do not spray during flowering. Apply fungicide in the evening or early morning. Wear protective clothing when handling chemicals.

### Brown Rust (Leaf Rust)
**Symptoms:**
- Small, round, orange-brown pustules scattered randomly on leaf surfaces
- Unlike yellow rust, pustules are not in stripes
- Severely affected leaves turn brown and die prematurely

**Cause:** Fungus *Puccinia triticina*. Favored by warmer temperatures (15–22°C).

**What to do:**
1. Monitor fields from February onwards
2. Spray if more than 10% of flag leaves are affected at booting stage
3. Apply propiconazole (Tilt 250EC) at 250 ml per acre
4. Ensure adequate potassium nutrition — potassium-deficient plants are more susceptible

**When:** February–March in Punjab and Sindh.

### Loose Smut
**Symptoms:**
- Infected plants produce black, powdery spore masses instead of grain heads
- The entire head is converted to black spores
- Often not noticed until heading stage

**Cause:** Seed-borne fungus *Ustilago tritici*. Infection occurs during flowering but symptoms appear at heading.

**What to do:**
1. Use certified, disease-free seed — this is the most effective control
2. Treat seed with carboxin (Vitavax) at 2.5g per kg seed before sowing
3. Rogue out (remove and destroy) infected plants when spotted in the field
4. If more than 5% of plants are infected, the field should not be used as seed source

**When:** Prevention at sowing time. No effective field treatment once symptoms appear.

**Caution:** Do not consume grain from heavily infected fields. Loose smut spores are not toxic but reduce grain quality significantly.

### Karnal Bunt
**Symptoms:**
- Partial or complete conversion of grain kernels to black, foul-smelling powder
- Affected grain has a fishy odor
- Only individual kernels are affected, not the whole head

**Cause:** Seed-borne fungus *Tilletia indica*. Spreads through infected seed and soil.

**What to do:**
1. Always use certified seed from reliable sources
2. Treat seed with propiconazole (Tilt) at 1 ml per kg seed
3. Avoid sowing in fields with known Karnal bunt history for at least 2 years
4. Clean harvested grain thoroughly — infected kernels are lighter and can be separated by gravity

**When:** Prevention at sowing. No field treatment available.

## Pests

### Aphids
**Symptoms:**
- Small green or black insects clustered on stems and leaves, especially near the base
- Leaves become sticky (honeydew secretion) and may develop sooty mold
- Severe infestation causes stunted growth and reduced grain filling

**What to do:**
1. Check fields weekly from tillering through grain filling
2. Natural enemies (ladybird beetles, syrphid flies) often control aphids — look for them before spraying
3. If more than 10 aphids per tiller are found and natural enemies are absent, spray insecticide
4. Apply imidacloprid (Confidor) at 80 ml per acre, or thiamethoxam (Actara) at 50 g per acre
5. Spray in the evening to minimize impact on beneficial insects

**When:** January–March. Peak populations in February.

**Caution:** Avoid broad-spectrum insecticides during flowering — they kill pollinators and natural enemies.

### Termites
**Symptoms:**
- Plants wilt and die in patches, especially near field boundaries
- Mud tubes visible on stems near soil surface
- Roots appear hollowed out

**What to do:**
1. Deep plough after harvest to expose termite colonies
2. Apply fipronil (Regent) granules at 10 kg per acre before sowing
3. Ensure proper field drainage — waterlogged areas attract termites
4. Remove crop debris and old stubble promptly after harvest

**When:** Prevention before sowing. Treat affected patches immediately when spotted.

## Common Problems

### Lodging (Plants Falling Over)
**Cause:** Excessive nitrogen, tall varieties, heavy irrigation during windy weather, high plant density.

**What to do:**
1. Do not exceed recommended nitrogen doses
2. Apply potassium fertilizer — strengthens stems
3. Avoid irrigation when strong winds (>30 km/h) are forecast
4. Use semi-dwarf varieties (most modern Pakistani varieties are semi-dwarf)

### Yellowing of Leaves (Chlorosis)
**Cause:** Most commonly zinc or iron deficiency in alkaline soils (widespread in Punjab). Can also indicate waterlogging or nitrogen deficiency.

**What to do:**
1. If yellowing is uniform across the field: likely nitrogen deficiency — top-dress with urea
2. If yellowing is in patches with stunted growth: likely zinc deficiency — foliar spray zinc sulphate (0.5% solution)
3. If leaves are yellow but veins remain green: likely iron deficiency — foliar spray ferrous sulphate (0.5% solution)
4. Check drainage — waterlogged roots cannot absorb nutrients

## Harvesting
- Harvest when grain moisture is 12–14% (grain is hard, not milky when bitten)
- Typically April–May in Punjab, March–April in Sindh
- Avoid harvesting during midday heat — grain shattering increases
- Thresh within 2–3 days of cutting to prevent field losses
- Store grain at moisture below 12% to prevent fungal growth and insect damage
- Use phosphine tablets for stored grain protection (1 tablet per tonne, sealed for 7 days)

## Local Measurements
- 1 maund (40 kg) wheat ≈ standard market unit
- 1 acre = 8 kanals = 160 marlas
- Seed rate: 50 kg per acre ≈ 1.25 maund per acre
